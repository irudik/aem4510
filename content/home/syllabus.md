## Course Information

**Course sections:** AEM 4510, AEM 5510, ECON 3865

**Professor:** Ivan Rudik (ir229)

This page provides the general course syllabus. See [Canvas](https://canvas.cornell.edu/) for the current course schedule, teaching team, office hours, assignment deadlines, exam logistics, and announcements.

**Prerequisites:** MATH 1110, and AEM 2600 or ECON 3030.

**Course description:** This class focuses on the role of the environment in the theory and practice of economics. It uses microeconomic analysis at the intermediate level and incorporates real-world examples. It examines market failures, externalities, benefit-cost analysis, non-market valuation techniques, cost-effective policy instruments, financial instruments, and recent applied work on the economics of the environment.

**Course requirements:** Students are expected to prepare for class, do the readings, and complete all problem sets and prelims.

## Readings and Lecture Slides

- *Markets and the Environment* by Keohane and Olmstead is required.
- Other readings are papers linked on the [course topics and lecture slides page](/lecture-notes/), or freely available through the [Cornell library](https://www.library.cornell.edu/) or [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/).
- [*Causal Inference: The Mixtape*](https://mixtape.scunning.com/) by Cunningham is free and useful for background on the econometric techniques used in readings in the second half of the course, or for students interested in environmental economics research.

## In-Class Rules

**No recording or sharing of materials outside class:** Each person in this class is expected to respect the principles of academic freedom for instructors and classmates and maintain the privacy of the classroom environment, as outlined in [Cornell's Code of Academic Integrity](https://deanoffaculty.cornell.edu/faculty-and-academic-affairs/academic-integrity/code-of-academic-integrity/). Members of this class will not record, photograph, or share online any interactions that involve classmates or any member of the teaching team. Students will also respect the intellectual property rights of the instructor and will not share or otherwise make accessible any course materials to anyone not enrolled in the course without the instructor's written permission.

This policy is not meant to restrict students' ability to use classroom recordings in ways beneficial to their learning. Students who may benefit from recorded lectures and lecture playback, including students who use English as an additional language or who have accommodations from Student Disability Services (SDS), should speak to the course instructor. Students approved to record lectures are expected to maintain the respect and privacy of the learning environment.

**No use of electronics in class:** The use of laptops, tablets, phones, or other electronics in class is prohibited. Notes must be taken on paper. Exceptions are available for those with specific accommodations. The reason for this rule is pedagogical: to improve learning in the course. Research has found that laptop or tablet use in class is associated with decreased learning for the user and those nearby, and that restrictions on computer use improved scores in controlled studies:

- Sana, F., Weston, T., & Cepeda, N. (2013). Laptop multitasking hinders classroom learning for both users and nearby peers. *Computers & Education*, 62, 24–31.
- Mueller, P., & Oppenheimer, D. (2014). The pen is mightier than the keyboard: Advantages of longhand over laptop note taking. *Psychological Science*, 25(6), 1159–1168.
- Carter, S. P., Greenberg, K., & Walker, M. S. (2017). The impact of computer usage on academic performance: Evidence from a randomized trial at the United States Military Academy. *Economics of Education Review*, 56, 118–132.

I will go through the material at an appropriate pace so that you can take high-quality notes. After each set of slides is completed in class, it will be posted on Canvas.

**In-class prelim and final exam rules:** If you need to leave to use the restroom, water fountain, or for another reason, you must leave your phone and other wearable electronics with a proctor. Bring your student ID or government photo ID and present it to a proctor when you turn in your exam.

## Grading

| Component | Share of course grade |
|---|---:|
| Four problem sets | 12% (3% each) |
| Four prelims | 68% (17% each) |
| Optional final exam | 20% |

If you have taken all four in-class prelims, you may skip the final and receive your average prelim grade as your final exam grade. If you miss a prelim, the final is required.

**Final course letter grades:** A (93%–100%), A− (90%–92%), B+ (87%–89%), B (83%–86%), B− (80%–82%), C+ (77%–79%), C (73%–76%), C− (70%–72%), D+ (67%–69%), D (63%–66%), D− (60%–62%), F (below 60%).

## Problem Sets

There are four problem sets, one before each prelim. You may consult with me or the teaching assistants if you have questions or need guidance. See Canvas for deadlines.

- You have unlimited submissions before the deadline. No late problem sets will be accepted because the answer key is posted immediately after the deadline. Aim to submit early enough to handle any problems.
- You may work in teams of up to three. Submit one problem set per team through Gradescope on Canvas, with all group members' names on it.
- If you work on paper, scan and convert your work to a PDF using a tool such as [Adobe Scan](https://www.adobe.com/acrobat/mobile/scanner-app.html). Pictures will not be accepted and will receive a zero.
- You are responsible for submitting the correct file. Unreadable or corrupted files will not be accepted. Your final submission before the deadline is what will be graded.

## Prelims and Final Exam

Prelims take place in class. See Canvas for the exam schedule and final exam logistics.

**Coverage:** Prelims cover all material taught before the exam, with a heavy focus on new content since the previous prelim. You remain responsible for knowing prior course material and how it builds into the new content. The final is cumulative. Questions are based on the slides and assigned readings.

**Permitted materials:** Bring a pen or pencil only. Electronics, calculators, and notes are not permitted. Paper will be provided.

**Review:** Problem sets are the main way to review for prelims. Answer keys are posted after each problem set deadline. Additional review questions will be posted on Canvas before each prelim. If time permits, some questions may be reviewed in class.

**Missed prelims:** There is no makeup for the first missed prelim. If you miss one prelim for any reason, its share of your course grade is transferred to the final, increasing the final's weight to 37%. The final is then required. Subsequent missed prelims with valid excuses require a makeup.

## Other Policies

**Attendance:** Class attendance is not explicitly required but is highly recommended.

**Grade appeals:** Submit appeals through Gradescope within the 24-hour appeal window. I reserve the right to regrade the entire assignment, and the new grade, higher or lower, will be final.

**Integrity of credit:** I expect every student to abide by the [Cornell University Code of Academic Integrity](https://cuinfo.cornell.edu/aic.cfm). I strongly encourage collaboration, but each student is responsible for following the rules in this syllabus and the Code of Academic Integrity. Any student found to have violated the stated policies on an assignment will receive a zero on that assignment. Violations may result in failure of the course.

## Learning Outcomes

As a result of participating in class, you will be able to:

- Discuss the design and effect of environmental policy in theory and real-world practice. AEM 5510 also covers the consequences of environmental policy in second-best environments.
- Use survey and revealed preference methods to value the environment.
- Explain how financial instruments provide information about the environment. AEM 5510 also covers how green financial instruments work.
- Explain how economists measure the economic costs of environmental change.
